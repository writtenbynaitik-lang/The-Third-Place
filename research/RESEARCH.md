## 3. Opening hours — how the conflict was resolved

Five sources disagreed. Resolution:

| Source | Says | Assessment |
|--------|------|------------|
| S5, S6 (Sept 2025 launch press) | Breakfast 8 am–12 pm; all day 12 pm–10:45 pm; dinner 7 pm–10:45 pm | **Launch-era.** Superseded — a year old, and contradicted by the restaurant's own current channels. |
| S3 Instagram (their own words) | "Open daily, 12:00 pm…" (truncated at the login wall) | Correct on the 12:00 pm open; closing time not readable. |
| S1 Zomato + S2 District, checked on a **Monday** | "Closed • Opens at 3:00 PM" | Correct that Monday opens later. |
| Search snippets | Mon 3:00 pm – 11:45 pm; Tue–Sun 12:00 pm – 11:45 pm | **Wrong on the closing time.** Third-hand. |
| **S8 Google Business profile** | **Mon 3–11 pm; Tue–Sun 12–11:30 pm** | **Adopted.** |

**Adopted: Monday 3:00 pm – 11:00 pm; Tuesday to Sunday 12:00 pm – 11:30 pm.**

S8 wins because it is the only source the business itself maintains that exposes
a full weekly schedule: the profile carries "From the owner" posts, the latest
published two days before this check. Note that Monday both **opens later and
closes earlier** than the rest of the week — a detail no other source captured.

12 October 2026 is a Monday, which is why Zomato, District and Google all showed
"opens at 3 pm" on the day of checking; the three agreeing is what first flagged
that Monday is different.

The earlier aggregator figure of 11:45 pm was carried into a draft of this site
and then corrected. It is recorded here so the error is not silently repeated.

## 5. Menu — read in full from 29 scanned pages (S7)

Two distinct printed menu systems are in circulation:

* **System A — current flagship.** Cream paper, vermilion typewriter-slab headings,
  monospace body, the `| | |` mark, `||| SECTION |||` dividers, vertical side tabs,
  allergen icon key, red square for non-vegetarian, "GST Applicable" footer.
  Covers the dinner menu and the whole bar/beverage list. **The website is built on
  this system.**
* **System B — daytime/all-day.** Sage-green geometric-sans headings, orange prices,
  corner brackets, "(V)" vegetarian markers, "THE THIRD PLACE" footer rule. Older or
  parallel. Noted, not used as the design basis.

### Editorial handling

* Dish **names and descriptions are reproduced as printed**, with four obvious
  typographic errors corrected: *Edammame→Edamame, pomogrenate→pomegranate,
  Brocolli→Broccoli, Chantly→Chantilly*. No meaning changed.
* **Prices are deliberately not displayed.** See Open question B.
* **No dietary claim is made.** System A marks non-vegetarian with a red square and
  carries an allergen key (dairy / gluten / nuts / crustaceans); the site reproduces
  that key as the menu's own, and makes no vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, organic or
  "healthy" claim of its own.
* The site states plainly that it shows a **selection**, not the full menu.

⚠️ **Open question B.** The menu scans carry no date. Prices (₹235–₹16,125) were
read but are **not published on the site** because currency could not be confirmed.
The figures are carried in `src/js/menu-data.js` alongside each dish; flipping
`showPrices` to `true` in that file publishes them, and nothing else changes.

⚠️ **Open question C.** The launch press (S4–S6) lists pizzas — Farmacy, Quattro
Formaggi, Caprese, Classic Pepperoni, Meat Overload — and Panuozzo sandwiches that
**do not appear** on the current menu scan. The scan is treated as newer. It is
independently corroborated: Zomato reviews name "Pinky Promise Pizza", "Caferal
Prawns" and "Gojju Smashed Potatoes", all of which are on the scan and none of which
are in the press release. Press-only dishes are not shown.

## 7. Distinguishing characteristics (what the design is built on)

1. **The name is a real idea, not decoration.** Ray Oldenburg's "third place": home
   is first, work is second, and the third is the public room where community
   happens. The founder states it outright (S4). This is rare — most restaurant names
   mean nothing.
2. **The mark is three strokes — `| | |`.** Confirmed on every menu page (S7) and as
   the Instagram avatar (S3). Three strokes for the third place. It works as logo,
   as `||| SECTION |||` divider, and as a structural rhythm.
3. **One room, two businesses.** A coffee house at noon and a wine bar at night, in
   the same room. Opening at noon and running to 11:30 pm makes the whole day
   the product.
4. **Indian wine, taken seriously.** The "Local Legends" page champions Nandi Hills,
   Nashik, Solapur and Akluj alongside Chablis and Sassicaia. That is a genuine point
   of view, not a list.
5. **The writing is already good.** "trust the wine"; "this one means what it says";
   "classic for a reason"; "simple things, done well". Plainspoken, warm, no
   pretension. The site adopts this voice rather than inventing one.
6. **Materials are honest.** Refurbished furniture, recycled materials, terrazzo,
   clay-render walls, exposed ducting, bentwood chairs, real daylight, real plants.
7. **It is more than a restaurant.** Retail corners, books, and recurring programming
   visible in the Instagram highlight names: "Saturday Muse", "Wed Theatre",
   "D'canted Wine".

⚠️ **Open question D.** "Saturday Muse", "Wed Theatre" and "D'canted Wine" are clearly
recurring events, but their content, timing and current status could not be verified
without logging in to Instagram. **They are not described on the site.** If they are
still running they deserve a section — ask the owners.
